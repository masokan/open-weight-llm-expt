The patient's presentation points strongly towards **C. Scleritis**.
Here's why:

*   **Deep boring pain:** This is a hallmark symptom of scleritis,
distinguishing it from conjunctivitis or episcleritis which typically
cause more superficial discomfort.
*   **Eye redness and photophobia:** These are common in inflammatory eye
conditions but can be present in scleritis as well.
*   **Pain on extraocular movement testing (EOM):** This suggests
inflammation of the sclera itself, affecting the muscles that control eye
movement.
*   **Positive lid squeeze test:** Pain with pressure over the eye is
characteristic of scleritis, indicating deep scleral involvement.
*   **History of rheumatoid arthritis and etanercept use:** Rheumatoid
arthritis is a known risk factor for secondary scleritis. Etanercept (a
TNF-alpha inhibitor) can also be associated with an increased risk of
developing inflammatory conditions like scleritis.
*   **Diminished visual acuity:** This suggests the inflammation is
significantly affecting the eye's function and structure.

Let's analyze why other options are less likely:

*   **A. Conjunctivitis:** Typically presents with redness, itching, and
discharge. Pain on EOM and lid squeeze test is not typical.
*   **B. Episcleritis:** Presents with milder symptoms of redness and mild
discomfort, but typically without the deep boring pain or positive lid
squeeze test seen in this case.
*   **D. Subconjunctival hemorrhage:** Usually presents as a bright red
patch on the white part of the eye without significant pain or tenderness.

Therefore, given the clinical presentation of severe eye pain, redness,
photophobia, and the presence of risk factors like rheumatoid arthritis
treated with etanercept, **scleritis** is the most likely diagnosis.
