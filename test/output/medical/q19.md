*   **A. Acute tubular necrosis (ATN):** ATN is a common cause of AKI,
especially in hospitalized patients. However, it often results from

ischemia and damage. The hematocrit being low also supports this diagnosis

*   **C. Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS):** HUS is typically associated
with a diarrheal prodrome caused by Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*. It's

decreased blood flow to the kidneys. While possible, there are no clear
signs of hypovolemia (warm, dry skin) and a history of hypertension makes
it less likely in this setting. Also, prerenal AKI does not typically
cause hematuria or granular casts unless there is underlying glomerular
disease

kidney injury.**
