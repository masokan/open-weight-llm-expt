   We need to find the antiderivative (indefinite integral) of the
function:
   $
   F(x) = \int \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{\cos x}{\sqrt{\sin x}} +
\frac{1}{x \sqrt{\ln x}} \right] \, dx.
   $
  
2. **Separate the Integral into Two Parts**:
   We can split the integral into two separate integrals:
   $
   F(x) = \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{\cos x}{\sqrt{\sin x}} \, dx +
\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{\ln x}}.
   $
  
3. **Solve the First Integral**:
   Consider the first part:
   $
   I_1 = \int \frac{\cos x}{\sqrt{\sin x}} \, dx.
   $
  
4. **Use Substitution for Simplification**:
   Let $ u = \sin x $. Then, \( du = \cos x \, dx \) and the integral
becomes:
   $
   I_1 = \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u}} = \int u^{-\frac{1}{2}} \, du.
   $
  
5. **Integrate**:
   The antiderivative of $ u^{-\frac{1}{2}} $ is:
   $
   I_1 = 2\sqrt{u} + C_1 = 2\sqrt{\sin x} + C_1,
   $
   where $ C_1 $ is an arbitrary constant.
  
6. **Solve the Second Integral**:
   Now consider the second part:
   $
   I_2 = \int \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{\ln x}}.
   $
  
7. **Use Substitution for Simplification**:
   Let $ v = \ln x $. Then, \( dv = \frac{1}{x} dx \) and the integral
becomes:
   $
   I_2 = \int \frac{dv}{\sqrt{v}} = \int v^{-\frac{1}{2}} \, dv.
   $
  
8. **Integrate**:
   The antiderivative of $ v^{-\frac{1}{2}} $ is:
   $
   I_2 = 2\sqrt{v} + C_2 = 2\sqrt{\ln x} + C_2,
   $
   where $ C_2 $ is an arbitrary constant.
  
9. **Combine the Results**:
   Combining the results from both integrals, we get:
   $
   F(x) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 2\sqrt{\sin x} + 2\sqrt{\ln x} \right) + C =
\sqrt{\sin x} + \sqrt{\ln x} + C.
   $
