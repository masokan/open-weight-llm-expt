To find the derivative of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{\sin(x)} +
\sqrt{\ln(x)}$, we will treat each term separately and apply the **Chain
Rule**.

The Chain Rule states that if you have a composite function $y = g(h(x))$,
its derivative is:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = g'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x)$$

### Step 1: Differentiate $\sqrt{\sin(x)}$
Let $u = \sin(x)$. The term becomes $\sqrt{u}$, or $u^{1/2}$.
The derivative of $u^{1/2}$ with respect to $u$ is $\frac{1}{2}u^{-1/2}$,
which is $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{u}}$.
Applying the chain rule:
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{\sin(x)}) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sin(x)}} \cdot
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin(x))$$
Since the derivative of $\sin(x)$ is $\cos(x)$:
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{\sin(x)}) = \frac{\cos(x)}{2\sqrt{\sin(x)}}$$

### Step 2: Differentiate $\sqrt{\ln(x)}$
Let $v = \ln(x)$. The term becomes $\sqrt{v}$, or $v^{1/2}$.
The derivative of $v^{1/2}$ with respect to $v$ is $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{v}}$.
Applying the chain rule:
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{\ln(x)}) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\ln(x)}} \cdot
\frac{d}{dx}(\ln(x))$$
Since the derivative of $\ln(x)$ is $\frac{1}{x}$:
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{\ln(x)}) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\ln(x)}} \cdot \frac{1}{x}
= \frac{1}{2x\sqrt{\ln(x)}}$$

### Step 3: Combine the results
Summing the derivatives of both parts gives the final derivative of
$f(x)$:

$$f'(x) = \frac{\cos(x)}{2\sqrt{\sin(x)}} + \frac{1}{2x\sqrt{\ln(x)}}$$
