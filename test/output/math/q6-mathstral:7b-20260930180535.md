Prompt: Prove that when two numbers of the form $a^2 + 3b^2$ are multiplied, the result is of the same form

1. Let the two numbers be $ A $ and \( B \), where \( A = a^2 + 3b^2 \)
and $ B = c^2 + 3d^2 $.

2. We need to show that the product $ A \times B $ can be expressed in
the form $ x^2 + 3y^2 $.

3. Calculate the product $ AB $:
   $
   AB = (a^2 + 3b^2)(c^2 + 3d^2)
   $

4. Expand this product using the distributive property:
   $
   AB = a^2 c^2 + a^2 \cdot 3d^2 + 3b^2 c^2 + 3b^2 \cdot 3d^2
   $

5. Simplify each term:
   $
   a^2 c^2 + 3a^2 d^2 + 3b^2 c^2 + 9b^2 d^2
   $

6. Group the terms in a way to express them as sums of squares:
   $
   AB = (ac)^2 + 3(ad)^2 + 3(bc)^2 + 9(bd)^2
   $

7. Recognize that we can rewrite $ 9(bd)^2 $ as \( 6(bd)^2 + 3(bd)^2 \):
   $
   AB = (ac)^2 + 3(ad)^2 + 3(bc)^2 + 6(bd)^2 + 3(bd)^2
   $

8. Notice that $ 6(bd)^2 $ can be written as \( 3(2bd)^2 \):
   $
   AB = (ac)^2 + 3(ad)^2 + 3(bc)^2 + 3(2bd)^2 + 3(bd)^2
   $

9. Combine the terms to show it is in the desired form:
   $
   AB = [(ac)^2 + 3(ad)^2 + 3(bc)^2 + 3(2bd)^2] + 3(bd)^2
   $

10. Observe that we can express this as a sum of squares and another
multiple of 3:
    $
    AB = (ac - 3bd)^2 + 3[(ad + bc)^2 + (2bd)^2]
    $

11. Thus, $ AB $ is in the form \( x^2 + 3y^2 \), where \( x = ac - 3bd
$ and $ y = ad + bc \).

### Conclusion:
$
\boxed{\text{The product of two numbers of the form } a^2 + 3b^2 \text{ is
also in the form } x^2 + 3y^2.}
$
