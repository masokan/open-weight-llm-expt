Prompt: What is the derivative of the function $f(x) = sqrt{sin(x)} + sqrt{ln(x)}$ ?

1. First, find the derivative of each term separately using the chain
rule.
\begin{align}
& \frac{d}{dx} [sqrt{sin(x)}] = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{sin(x)}} * cos(x) \\
& \frac{d}{dx} [sqrt{ln(x)}] = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{ln(x)}} * \frac{1}{x}
\end{align}

2. Now, combine the derivatives found in step 1 and simplify if necessary.
$
f'(x) = \frac{cos(x)}{2\sqrt{sin(x)}} + \frac{1}{2x\sqrt{ln(x)}}
$

So, the derivative of $f(x)$ is $\boxed{\frac{cos(x)}{2\sqrt{sin(x)}} +
\frac{1}{2x\sqrt{ln(x)}}}$.
