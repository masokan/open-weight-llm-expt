To solve the differential equation $x^2 \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + x
\frac{dy}{dx} = 49y$, we recognize that it is a **Cauchy-Euler equation**
(or Euler-Cauchy equation).

### 1. Identify the type of equation
A Cauchy-Euler equation takes the form:
$$ax^2 \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + bx \frac{dy}{dx} + cy = f(x)$$
In your equation, $a=1$, $b=1$, and $c=-49$. Since the power of $x$
matches the order of the derivative in each term, we can use the
characteristic equation method.

### 2. Assume a solution form
For Cauchy-Euler equations, we assume a solution of the form:
$$y = x^r$$
Taking the derivatives:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = rx^{r-1}$$
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = r(r-1)x^{r-2}$$

### 3. Substitute into the original equation
Substitute $y, \frac{dy}{dx},$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ into the
differential equation:
$$x^2(r(r-1)x^{r-2}) + x(rx^{r-1}) = 49x^r$$
Simplify the powers of $x$:
$$r(r-1)x^r + rx^r = 49x^r$$
Factor out $x^r$ (assuming $x \neq 0$):
$$r(r-1) + r = 49$$
$$r^2 - r + r = 49$$
$$r^2 = 49$$
