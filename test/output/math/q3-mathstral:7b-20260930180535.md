Prompt: What is the antiderivative of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{\sin(x)} + \sqrt{\ln(x)}$ ?

1. To find an antiderivative of the given function $ f(x) = \sqrt{\sin x}
+ \sqrt{\ln x} $, we start by using the substitution method, particularly
the trigonometric substitution for $ \sqrt{\sin x} $.

2. Let's denote:
   $
   t^2 = \frac{1 - \cos x}{2}
   $
   From this, it follows that:
   $
   t^2 + \frac{\cos x}{2} = \frac{1}{2}
   $
   Therefore:
   $
   2t^2 = 1 - \cos x
   $
   Taking the differential of both sides:
   $
   4t \, dt = -\sin x \, dx \implies \sin x \, dx = -4t \, dt
   $

3. Now substitute $ t^2 = \frac{1 - \cos x}{2} $ and \( \sin x \, dx =
-4t \, dt $ into the integral:
   $
   I_1 = \int \sqrt{\sin x} \, dx = \int (1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} (-4t \, dt)
   $
   Note that we are dealing with an improper integral, and thus, $ t $
ranges from $-\infty$ to \(+\infty\).

4. Splitting the improper integral into two parts:
   $
   I_1 = -4 \int_{0}^{\infty} (1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} t \, dt + 4
\int_{-\infty}^{0} (1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} t \, dt
   $

5. To handle the symmetry, observe that:
   $
   I_1 = -4 \int_{0}^{\infty} (1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} t \, dt + 4
\int_{0}^{\infty} (-t)(1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} \, dt
   $
   Combining the integrals:
   $
   I_1 = -8 \int_{0}^{\infty} (1 - 2t^2)^{1/4} t \, dt
   $
   Let $ u = 1 - 2t^2 $. Then, \( du = -4t \, dt \) and integrating with
respect to $ t $:
   $
   I_1 = 8 \int_{1}^{0} \frac{u^{3/4}}{4} \, (-du) = 2 \int_{0}^{1}
u^{3/4} \, du
   $
   The integral evaluates to:
   $
   2 \left[ \frac{u^{7/4}}{7/4} \right]_0^1 = 2 \cdot \frac{4}{7} \cdot 1
= \frac{8}{7}
   $

6. For the second integral $ I_2 $:
   $
   I_2 = \int \sqrt{\ln x} \, dx = \text{let} \, z^2 = \ln x \implies x =
e^{z^2}
   $
   Differentiating:
   $
   dx = 2ze \, dt = 2z e^{z^2} \, dz
   $
   Substituting $ x $ and \( dx \):

   $

   $
   2 \left[ z \cdot \frac{e^{z^2}}{2} - \int \frac{e^{z^2}}{2} \, dz
\right] = 2 \left[ \frac{ze^{z^2}}{2} - \frac{e^{z^2}}{2} \right]
   $
   Evaluating the expression:
   $
   2 \left( \frac{ze^{z^2} - e^{z^2}}{2} \right) = (ze^{z^2} - e^{z^2}) =
e^{z^2}(z - 1)
   $
   Converting back to $ x $:
   $
   I_2 = e^{\ln x}(\sqrt{\ln x} - 1) = x(\sqrt{\ln x} - 1)
   $

   $
   F(x) = \frac{8}{7} + x (\sqrt{\ln x} - 1) + C
